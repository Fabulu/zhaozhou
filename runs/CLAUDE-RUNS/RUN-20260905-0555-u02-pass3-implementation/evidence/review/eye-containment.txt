THE STAR AND THE LENS -- measured on the comparison side, after looking.

A. IT DOES NOT BREAK THE SILHOUETTE (my own first read, refuted)

Looking at plate 03 at 8x I called the far star "floating on the sky outside
the ink". I then flood-filled the background from the frame border, stopping
at the cel ink, and counted star pixels landing in it:

  u02-s4-front  cyan 278 px   cyan outside the ink silhouette:  0
  u02-s4-tq     cyan 194 px   cyan outside the ink silhouette:  0
  u02-s4-side   cyan  28 px   cyan outside the ink silhouette:  0

The eye assembly stands proud of the body and the ink wraps around it, so the
star stays inside the drawn outline. My first impression was wrong. Recorded.

B. IT DOES BREAK THE LENS

Star pixels 8-adjacent to bare flesh or to ink -- i.e. running to the lens rim
rather than sitting inside a white ring:

  u02-s4-front  104 of 278  (37%)
  u02-s4-tq      75 of 194  (39%)
  u02-s4-side    20 of  28  (71%)

And plate 05 (curious, the four gaze extremes) shows it plainly at the
shipping camera: on two of the four the far star sits almost entirely OFF its
lens, on bare pink flesh, with the white left behind as a crescent beside it.

C. THE MECHANISM, FROM THE SHIPPED CONSTANTS

  unnamed02_art.h
    kEyeWideMm        125   lens half-width
    kPupilStarArmMm   185   star arm half-length
    kEyeBulgeMm        88   the star stands this far proud of the lens
    kGazeMaxA16      3000   gaze sweep, uncapped by any lens-space clamp

  185 > 125. The star's arm is HALF AGAIN the lens's half-width, so across the
  lens's short axis the star overhangs the rim by 60 mm before the gaze moves
  at all. No clamp can contain it; the geometry cannot fit. Growing the star
  toward the sheet's ~20% area share (plan R3) was right, but it was grown
  against the lens's LONG axis while the short axis was left at 125.

  Second mechanism: mku02page.py paints the white ring "TIGHT around where the
  star rests (the outward station)" -- i.e. at ONE fixed place on the lens
  page -- while the star is separate geometry riding the pupil bone. The
  moment the gaze moves, the star leaves its ring. That is why the white reads
  as a rim arc on the lens instead of a ring around the star: it is a ring
  around where the star USED to be.

D. THE SIDE READ

  cyan pixels visible:  front 278   three-quarter 194   side 28

  At 90 deg the star is at 10% of its frontal presence -- 28 pixels in a 5x16
  slit (plate 04). Direction 3 asked for the eyes to read from the side "as
  the side sheet shows", where the eye is the largest feature on the bulb. The
  partial yaw restored in pass 3 (kEyeYawOutA16 2400) plus the dome
  (kEyeDeepMm 52 -> 90) improved the three-quarter read, which is the shipping
  camera and is the important one -- but at true profile the eye is still a
  purple stripe with a blue edge, not an eye.
