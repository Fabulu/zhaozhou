PRESENTATION SEAM (tools/reel/wrapseam.py): the mean absolute inter-frame
difference across the clip, with the wrap frame called out against the clip's
OWN interior median. This is the half that mqa's Q3 cannot see, because Q3
reads the AUTHORED KEYS and this reads the rendered frames.

=== PASS 25 (before) ===
bank25     n=240   interior median 0.707   p95 0.922   MAX interior 0.989 at f211
             INTO the wrap frame  f237->f238     0.577  =   0.82x median  in band
             out of the wrap      f238->f239     1.206  =   1.71x median

=== PASS 26 (after) ===
bank26     n=240   interior median 3.832   p95 4.723   MAX interior 5.383 at f231
             INTO the wrap frame  f237->f238     2.408  =   0.63x median  in band
             out of the wrap      f238->f239    15.019  =   3.92x median

READ IT WITH P26-RECEIPTS/loop-seam.txt, NOT INSTEAD OF IT. The two answer
different questions and they disagree in an informative way:

  * loop-seam.txt asks WHERE THE CREATURE IS -- the pass-25 receipt's own
    metric, the pink-ink centroid at f0 vs the last frame. 38.8 px -> 0.0 px.
  * wrapseam.py asks HOW MUCH THE PICTURE CHANGES at the wrap, whole frame,
    creature and terrain together. Its ratio rises, because the TERRAIN now
    snaps back a full traverse where before it sat still.

So the creature's seam vanished and the ground's appeared. That trade is the
point of clocking the aim by the animation's phase, and it is the right way
round: the owner accepted a hitch on a clip that traverses, and a featureless
desert shifting is far less visible than a creature jumping a tenth of frame.
The interior median also rises (0.707 -> 3.9) simply because the clip now
MOVES -- a wrap ratio is stated against the clip's own motion for exactly
that reason, and the absolute interior figure is the honest one to compare.
