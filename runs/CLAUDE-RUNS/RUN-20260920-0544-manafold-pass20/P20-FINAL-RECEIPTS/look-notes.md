# Pass-20 final bank: look notes

Written after each look, before the next image was requested. Sheets are
`P20-BANK-SHEETS/` (96x60 tiles, every frame, index on each, <=1600 px JPEG
q80). Close looks are `P20-FINAL-LOOKS/`.

**What sheet scale is.** At 1600 px the Inspect/Hover sheets put 20 tiles per
row at about 78 px wide -- a fifth of native width. That scale can judge frame
presence, silhouette continuity, framing, gross pose and gross colour. It
CANNOT judge the rear joint, the ball ranking, line weight or the particle
reaction. Those are the close looks, and each says which bank it is against.

---

## 1. Inspect (600 frames) -- whole sheet

All 600 tiles present, contiguous, indexed 0..599. No blank, black, or
half-drawn frame. The orbit runs continuously: the creature turns through a
full revolution, the key light swings with it (pink front through violet back
to pink), and the ground horizon stays put.

Silhouette continuity: no pop anywhere along a row or across a row break. The
antenna's long diagonal presentation through f300-f339 -- the frames where the
loop is closest to side-on and the rear run is most exposed -- arrives and
leaves smoothly; that window is where pass 19's tear was visible, so it is the
first close look.

Nothing at this scale reads as broken. No judgement yet on the rear joint, the
ball ranking or the particles: too small.

## 2. Hover (600 frames) -- whole sheet

All 600 tiles present and contiguous. Continuous through the loop and across
the seam: f599 and f0 hold the same pose, key and framing. The bob reads as a
bob -- the horizon rides up through f60-f99 and back down -- with no step at any
row break. The long side-on antenna presentation through f300-f320 arrives and
leaves cleanly, as on Inspect.

Around f160-f200 the loop's top silhouette sits visibly lower and flatter than
its neighbours. That is where the knead should be, so it is the second close
look; at 78 px I can see a change in the top of the shape but not the ball
ranking, and I am not calling it from here.

No blank, black, torn or half-drawn frame.

## 3. Drift (300 frames) -- whole sheet

All 300 tiles present and contiguous. The creature crosses from the left and
approaches, so it grows across the clip; the horizon is steady and the framing
never clips it. Motion is smooth tile to tile with no jump at any row break,
and the mana reads as a small bright cluster rather than the version-18 blob.

Drift is the distance case, so sheet scale here is even further from a verdict
than usual: the creature is roughly 20-40 px on a 78 px tile. Presence,
framing, continuity only. The line weight and the particle reaction go to a
close look against the dip-off bank.

## 4. Channel (420 frames) -- whole sheet

All 420 tiles present and contiguous. The night scene is kept: moon, violet
sky, blue lightning figure over the pink body. The figure's shape changes
continuously -- no frame where it blinks out, doubles or snaps to a different
form -- and the body and horizon are steady. f080, the frame the re-review used
for the rear join, sits in an unremarkable stretch at this scale.

## 5. Taunt III / nodule taunt (368 frames) -- whole sheet

All 368 tiles present and contiguous (the last row is the expected 8 of 20,
padded black by the sheet tool, not missing frames). This is the clip the owner
named as the reference for the kneading motion, and the crown shuffle reads: the
nodules travel around the loop through f100-f200, with one carrier clearly
running low on the left. Continuous throughout; no pop at any row break.

## 6. Crackle (600 frames) -- whole sheet

All 600 tiles present and contiguous. Day sky kept, orbit continuous, framing
steady. No blank or torn frame.

## 7. Death II / death-gutter (590) + Hits / damage (464) -- composite, ~46 px tiles

The coarsest look of the set, and the weakest: framing and continuity only.
Both subjects are complete and contiguous (590 and 464 tiles, the trailing

## 8. Death / death-drop (450 frames) -- whole sheet

## 9. Trick (400) + Rest (400) -- composite, ~62 px tiles

## 10. Flight (352) + Mana lasso (336) + Fall (340) -- composite, ~47 px tiles

## 11. Blown (292) + Taunt (280) + Lasso / taunt2 (240) -- composite, ~60 px tiles

## 12. Hasty (240) + Pirouette (240) + Curious (180) + Startle (160) + Hit (140)

All five complete and contiguous, 960 tiles in one image. Hasty streaks across

**Sheet pass complete: 22 of 22 subjects, 7,992 of 7,992 frames looked at, no

The three things the pass is FOR -- the rear join, B reaching the bottom, and

# Close looks

## 13. The rear join, whole frames at 2x -- Inspect f380 and Channel f080,
shipping against the pass-19 bank (`CLOSE-1b-REAR-WHOLE-2X.jpg`)

These are the two frames the re-review named. At 2x the whole creature is
readable and the difference is already visible on both:

* **Inspect f380.** In pass 19 the band that runs down into the body on the
  right narrows as it goes and meets the body at a hard corner, with the loop
  pulled in tight around it. In pass 20 the same band stays broad all the way
  down and the loop sits wider and rounder. It reads as the band having been
  allowed to bow outwards instead of being pulled short.
* **Channel f080.** Pass 19's right-hand descent is a thin stalk with a kink
  where it enters the body. Pass 20's is a thicker trunk that widens into the
  body.

Framing, palette, night sky, moon, motes and the front crystals are unchanged
on both -- the difference is confined to the rear run, which is what the scope
table says too.

## 14. The rear join at 6x -- Channel f080 (`CLOSE-2-REAR-JOIN-6X.jpg`)

The exact frame the re-review measured at 361 mm hand-off / 113 deg.

**Pass 20: one continuous fused trunk.** The ink outline comes down the band's
right side and sweeps out into the body's shoulder in a single unbroken curve.
There is no separate edge and no corner; the junction reads as a rounded
shoulder, and the band's own width is constant right into it.

**Pass 19: a pinched step.** The band is visibly narrower, it constricts
further just above the junction, and the two outlines meet at a hard V instead
of a curve. That constriction is the thing the owner was describing.

## 15. The rear join at 5x -- Inspect f380 (`CLOSE-2b-REAR-JOIN-INSPECT-5X.jpg`)

The same verdict on the clip the owner named. Pass 19 shows a splayed wedge:
the band's edge and the body's edge cross at an angle and leave a notch with a
step in the fill. Pass 20 has neither -- the band arrives wide and the two
edges join smoothly. **The rear connection no longer tears or over-stretches,
and it is visible rather than inferred.**

(One earlier crop, `CLOSE-1-...-3X.jpg`, was aimed at the wrong part of the
frame and showed only body and band. It is kept because a look that found
nothing is still a look, and because the reason it found nothing was the crop
box, not the creature.)

## 16. The knead at NATIVE 384x240 (`CLOSE-3-KNEAD-NATIVE.jpg`)

**The frames were chosen by BADNESS, not by index** (`dipframes.py`,
`dipframes.txt`): the per-frame pixel difference between the shipping bank and
the `rearonly` control is the size of the beat's effect, and the largest is the
deepest press. Inspect f280 (16,654 px of 92,160), Hover f532 (12,828).

Eight tiles at native, no magnification at all, because "does the owner's
sentence read on the page" is a native-resolution question:

| | |
|---|---|
| Inspect, beat off, f280 | the loop is a tall, open, rounded arch standing clear above the body |
| Inspect, shipping, f250 | loop up and open |
| **Inspect, shipping, f280** | **the top of the loop is pressed into a deep notch** -- the middle of the top run is driven down almost to the body while the two shoulders stay up as peaks |
| Inspect, shipping, f310 | loop up and open again |
| Hover, beat off, f532 | open arch, standing high |
| Hover, shipping, f500 / f532 / f560 | up, **pressed down and flattened onto the body**, up again |

**The gesture reads at 240p**: up, press, up. It is not a wobble and not a
spasm -- the pressed frame is unmistakably a different, deliberate shape, and
the frames either side are the ordinary idle. This is the thing the first
review called "not legible" and it is legible now.

## 17. The loop top at 3x (`CLOSE-5-KNEAD-LOOPTOP-3X.jpg`)

Inspect f280 and Hover f532, shipping against the beat off.

With the beat off, the top of the loop is a clean arch: it runs from the left
shoulder up and across to the right corner with no dip in it. With the beat on,
the same run carries a **deep trough in the middle** -- the middle-top ball has
been driven down well below the line joining its two neighbours, and the ink
outline follows it down into a V. On Hover the whole loop is lowered onto the
body as well as pressed.

**What this look can and cannot say.** It shows, plainly, that the middle-top
ball presses down and becomes the low point of the loop. It CANNOT settle the
full 3D ranking -- "strictly lower than every other ball, including the outer
ones sitting against the body" is a depth question, and a silhouette at 3x does
not answer it. That claim rests on R5 (19 of 19 hosting clips, worst margin
+37 mm), re-measured independently in the re-review. I am not restating it from
a picture.

## 18. The mana reacting, inside the loop (`CLOSE-4-KNEAD-LOOP-3X.jpg`)

Aimed at the loop's interior rather than its top, and it turned out to be the
clearest reaction evidence of the set. At the press, the lightning strands sit
**lower and more spread** than with the beat off, on both Inspect and Hover --
the figure is flattened and pushed sideways rather than simply agitated. The
motes go with it. Same palette, same count, same distance-scaled line weight:
the mana is being squeezed, not restyled.

## 19. The reaction on the other clips (`CLOSE-6-REACTION-2X.jpg`)

Drift f165, Channel f212 and Blown f166, shipping over beat-off, at 2x.

* **Channel: clear.** The loop is pressed down and the lightning figure rides
  down and flattens with it, spread wider across the loop's interior. Night
  sky, moon and the front crystals are identical.
* **Drift: visible in the pair, subtle alone.** The mana cluster flattens and
  spreads; at that distance it is a few pixels of shape. Correctly small --
  this is the far clip, and a big reaction there would be wrong.
* **Blown: at the limit of what this look can call.** The creature is small and
  tumbling; the measured difference is real (3,549 px) but at 2x I can see a
  small change in the loop and its motes and would not claim more. The one-shots
  are the weakest read of the beat, which is a property of those clips -- the
  loop is small and partly out of frame -- not of the beat.

**No new fault found.** Nothing in the bank reads as torn, stretched, popped or
missing, and the two things the owner asked for are on screen.
