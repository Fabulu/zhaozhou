# DSP audit — the 171 census does not describe this console

**Received 2026-08-21 from the owner**, audited against `main` at `7f2d588`.
Filed because its central correction invalidates the plan that was in
`design/budgets/dsp.md`, and because the arithmetic transformations below are
exact and reusable.

**Every new number here is a PAPER ESTIMATE requiring a per-block fit.** The
algebraic transformations are exact unless marked speculative. Nothing in this
document has been synthesised, fitted, or run on a board.

---

## 1. The correction that matters most

The `171 -> ~133` plan rests on `reports/synthesis/zhao_block_fit.json`, and
that file says of itself:

    sourceCommit  : 96c0394a768519453b80d68998a670dd59f9aeb5
    rtlCleanAtHead: false

**It is 93 commits old and was measured against a tree that was not clean at
HEAD.** Verified 2026-08-21.

Worse than the audit assumed. Checked module by module: the census measures
**42 modules; the repository contains 88**. Forty-six have never been fitted at
all, and they include the largest multiplier consumers written since:

| Never measured | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| `zhao_geom_project` | duplicates TERRAIN.PROJECT's nine 32x32 matrix products |
| `zhao_geom_skin` | its own header calls it the largest multiplier count in the repo |
| `zhao_geom_mat3x4_mul`, `zhao_geom_quat2mat`, `zhao_geom_pose_decode` | pose and matrix maths |
| the entire Field IR engine | alu, rcp, sin, len, normalize, curve, noise, rot, ring, seq |
| `zhao_part_expand`, `zhao_part_soft`, `zhao_terrain_velocity`, `zhao_texture_aux`, `zhao_texture_mosaic` | unfitted |

So:

- **133 is plausible for the old measured subset after the logged cuts.**
- **It is not a credible full-console total**, and nothing should be planned
  from it.

`design/budgets/dsp.md` said per-block fits are an upper bound. The sharper
problem is the opposite one: **the census is also an UNDER-count of the
console**, because half the console is not in it.

## 1b. VERIFIED AGAINST THE SHIPPED RTL (2026-08-21)

Every claim below was checked against the code before any of it was acted on.
The audit survives, but four things change and one claim is wrong.

### TERRAIN.LOD — understated, and the error helps

- **The throughput target is one decision per PATCH per frame, not per
  subpatch.** `TERRAIN.LOD.md:160` and the block header both say so. The audit
  is **16x off**, and measured headroom is **~11x** (~2,900 patches/frame
  against 256 required). Serialisation is therefore far MORE affordable than
  the audit argued.
- The ladder is not "multiple products". `ladder_ok()` holds two multiplies,
  `ladder()` calls it three times, and four `ladder()` calls sit in one
  `always_comb`: **12 dev·scale + 12 distance·threshold = 24 multiplier
  instances**, folding to roughly 6 + 6 real ones after constant folding and
  CSE.
- The distance squares are **33x33, not 32x32** (`diff33` sign extension
  forces it), and they are **UNGATED**: `cam0_en_i`/`cam1_en_i` gate only the
  selection, so a single-camera frame still pays all six.
- All 24 ladder multipliers hang off `sq_res` with **no enable**, so they
  re-evaluate on every one of the 32 square-root cycles to produce a value read
  once in `StDecide`.
- The isqrt is **inlined, not `zhao_field_isqrt`**, is compare-subtract only,
  and consumes **zero multipliers**. It is latency, not DSP.

**And the contract is wrong about its own block.** `TERRAIN.LOD.md`'s synthesis
section lists "four 49-bit ladder comparators" and **no ladder multipliers**;
the RTL has twelve comparators and twenty-four multiplies. The header at
`:41-43` claims "ONE comparator serves both" ladders; the RTL replicates. Same
shape as the `OP_ABS` defect — a document and an implementation disagreeing,
with nothing checking.

### SURFACE.STAMP — one claim needs repair before use

- **The progression is exact on `numx`, but `numx` is NOT the coordinate.** It
  is a pre-division numerator and the divide is **truncation toward zero**,
  which is **not distributive over addition**. `trunc128()` tests the sign bit
  and a non-zero remainder and adds one back. So the recurrence removes the
  multiply but the fixup must still run **every texel**. "Two multipliers become
  two adders" is not the whole story.
- The radius squares are **not** computed in parallel with texel work: they are
  latched in `SIdle` at command accept, strictly before the loop. But they are
  **64x64 signed and permanently live**, toggling for all 4,102 cycles of a
  stamp to produce a value consumed once.
- **`numz` is a SECOND row-invariant multiply** the audit missed, hoistable by
  the same argument as `dz^2`.
- **`dx^2`/`dz^2` are 64x64 where 31x31 would do.** The +/-4,096 m domain bounds
  |dx| at 2^30; the contract calls the width "honestly oversized", chosen to
  match the reference `int64` bit-for-bit. **Narrowing these is probably a
  bigger win than the hoist**, and the audit does not mention it.
- Nothing is coverage-gated: a stamp covering one texel pays the same
  multiplier toggle count as one covering 4,096.
- A full stamp is **4,102 cycles**, not 4,096.

**`SURFACE.STAMP.md:403-423` already documents most of §2.2**, including the
incremental form, explicitly recorded as *not attempted*. The audit re-derives
a known position there. `TERRAIN.LOD` has no equivalent analysis, which is
where the audit's real value lies.

## 2. The exact cuts the logged plan missed

Ordered by size. Each is exact integer algebra unless marked.

### 2.1 TERRAIN.LOD: 28 -> 4-8 DSP (the clearest omission)

Not a pixel-rate or even vertex-rate unit. Its target is **one LOD decision per
subpatch per frame**, and it already spends 32 sequential cycles in its
integer-square-root machinery. Yet it computes X2/Y2/Z2 for **both** cameras,
plus deviation x scale and distance x threshold products, largely in parallel.

> Upside-down resource allocation: the surrounding block is already sequential,
> but its multipliers are parallel.

Redesign: one 33x33 signed multiplier walked across every product, or two (one
lane per camera). Three cycles for dx2/dy2/dz2 per camera, then the existing
square root. `dev[level] x scale` computed once. **The strict comparison is
`distance x 256`, which is a shift.** The relaxed comparison computes
`distance x hysteresis` once and reuses it for all levels — `ladder()` currently
recomputes related products across comparisons.

| Design | Est. DSP | Added work |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| current | 28 | — |
| two shared lanes | ~8 | ~7 extra multiply-issue cycles |
| one shared lane | ~4 | ~14 extra cycles |

**~20-24 DSP saved, no arithmetic or behavioural change.**

### 2.2 SURFACE.STAMP: 28 -> 4-8 DSP (not just the radius squares)

The logged plan attacks only the two radius squares (~8). The stronger redesign
removes almost every multiplier.

**Delete both span multipliers.** `numx(i) = spanx * (2i+1)` is an arithmetic
progression:

    numx(0) = spanx ;  numx(i+1) = numx(i) + 2*spanx
    numz(0) = spanz ;  numz(j+1) = numz(j) + 2*spanz

Walk an accumulator and shift, preserving the negative-span truncation
correction exactly. Two wide multipliers become two adders.

**One shared square lane for everything else**: outer_radius^2 and
inner_radius^2 at command setup, dz^2 once per row (Z is constant across a row),
dx^2 once per texel.

    2 setup + 64 row-Z + 4096 texel-X = 4162 multiply cycles

against a scan that already touches 4096 texels: **+66 slots, about 1.6%**,
before existing memory stalls.

**~20-24 DSP saved**, against the 8 already logged — a further **12-16**.

### 2.3 TEXTURE.TMU: 28 -> 8-12, or 3-5 at current throughput

The bilinear unit forms all four weights and multiplies all four texels,
instantiated per channel: 7 DSP each, 28 for four. Factorise:

    top    = (t00 << 8) + (t10 - t00) * fu
    bottom = (t01 << 8) + (t11 - t01) * fu
    acc    = (top << 8) + (bottom - top) * fv
    out    = round_half_up(acc / 65536)

Algebraically identical to the four-weight form, **with exactly one final
rounding and no intermediate interpolation rounded** — so it preserves the
repository's bilerp law. **8 variable products per channel becomes 3.**

- future one-sample-per-clock: 4 channels x 3 = 12 small multipliers, **~8-12 DSP**;
- **at CURRENT throughput** (one direct sample per 4 clocks, one CLUT per 6), a
  single channel engine can walk R/G/B/A on successive clocks while the
  request/cache machinery fetches the next footprint: **~3-5 DSP**.

The second is the "make it fit now" configuration.

**Verify by exhaustive C++ equivalence over all fu/fv and texel extremes FIRST,
then make that the RTL differential oracle.**

### 2.4 GEOM.BINNER + GEOM.SETUP: 16 -> 4 DSP

The binner's 12 DSPs multiply wide edge coefficients by **tile indices of about
0-23** — a six-bit integer, once per triangle.

The best route is architectural. GEOM.SETUP already uses four products for kc0
and kc1 and derives kc2 from the area identity. Compute the edge values at the
first tile origin directly:

    E0_start = orient(B, C, Pstart)
    E1_start = orient(C, A, Pstart)
    E2_start = area2 - E0_start - E1_start     // free, same identity

Four products total; pass E_start, kx, ky and rnz to the binner, which then only
adds while walking tiles. **16 -> 4, no precision loss, no extra per-reference
work.** Fallback: force the tiny-coordinate products into a shift/add network
explicitly (do not rely on inference), trading DSP for ALMs.

### 2.5 RASTER.FRAGMENT: 10 -> ~7 DSP (immediate cheap win)

Each blend channel computes `(src-dst)*alpha` **and** `src*alpha` in parallel
though the mode can use only one. One signed product suffices:

    mul_left = (mode == ALPHA) ? signed(src - dst) : signed(zext(src))
    product  = mul_left * alpha

**2 -> 1 DSP per channel, 3 channels, ~3 DSP saved. No added cycle, no
throughput reduction, no semantic change.**

### 2.6 TERRAIN.TESS: the variable 17th morph bit (fitter-dependent)

The 17th bit exists only because `morph == 65536` (exactly 1.0) is legal. Both
endpoints can bypass the multiply — `morph == 0` returns the original height,
65536 the coarse height — leaving a 16x34 product instead of 17x34.
**Perhaps 2-4 DSP, genuinely fitter-dependent.**

## 3. The uncounted arrivals

These are not savings against 171. They are **new DSP that has never been
counted and must not be integrated in its current parallel form.**

- **GEOM.PROJECT duplicates TERRAIN.PROJECT** — its own header calls the
  duplication a cost, not a feature, and it carries another nine 32x32 products.
  Do not allow two full parallel projectors into the composed machine: extract
  one shared projector and arbitrate terrain and creature vertices, or serialise
  to ~12 DSP each.
- **GEOM.SKIN** forms 18 full 32x32 products plus six weight products. Exact
  reduction, no rounding change:

      blend = w0*pa + (64 - w0)*pb   ==   (pb << 6) + w0*(pa - pb)

  Two weight multiplies per row become one. Then three shared 32x32
  multipliers, one per output row: ~6 cycles for a dual-weight vertex, ~3 for a
  rigid one, ~12 DSP instead of dozens. The wider opportunity is to share one
  multiplier bank across pose-cache decode, skinning and projection — all
  32-bit fixed-point matrix work at different scheduling rates.
- **Field IR needs a shared multiply SERVICE, not a multiplier per op family.**
  `zhao_field_alu` forms MUL/MAD, DOT2 and DOT3 products in parallel, but the
  sequencer already reads operands over three states, so one multiplier maps
  naturally onto Q_RD0/1/2 with an accumulator building DOT2/DOT3 — possibly at
  no added latency if pipelined.

  `zhao_field_rcp` performs two physical multiplications in a combinational
  block, and the sequencer deliberately makes RCP cost one state like ADD. That
  is the expensive reading of "the sequencer owns the pipeline". Make RCP
  multi-cycle through the shared multiplier.

  Each op family has individually done the right thing by walking one local
  multiplier — but **one multiplier per family becomes many physical multipliers
  once they all sit beside a sequencer that can execute only one opcode at a
  time.** The shape wanted is:

      FIELD.SEQ
        +- shared 32x32 multiply service
        +- shared reciprocal support
        +- shared sine/table memories
        +- op-specific control state

  Also question whether **five physical profile sequencers** are needed. Five
  language profiles do not require five engines if measured demand allows one or
  two.

> **The machine is not inherently a 171-DSP architecture. It is a design in
> which many low-rate or mutually exclusive products were described in
> parallel.**

## 5. The order of work

1. **Refresh the per-block census at current HEAD**, including every new RTL
   block even if not yet integrated. **No more planning from `96c0394`.**
2. One-multiplier-per-channel blend (2.5) — tiny, exact, easy to measure.
3. SURFACE.STAMP numerator accumulators plus one shared square lane (2.2).
4. Serialise TERRAIN.LOD onto one or two lanes (2.1).
5. Factorised bilerp, proven exhaustively equivalent first (2.3).
6. GEOM.SETUP to BINNER first-tile edge values (2.4).
7. Extract a shared projector before GEOM.PROJECT enters the composed machine.
8. Replace GEOM.SKIN's parallel form before fitting it.
9. Build the shared Field multiply service before wiring the remaining op
   families.
10. New composed fit, and only then decide whether 1-3 DSP cuts in TESS, CLIP
    and EDGEWALK are worth their latency.

## 6. The real discovery

Not one stray multiplier. **LOD, STAMP, TMU, skinning and Field IR all have
scheduling slack or mutual exclusion that the present RTL does not convert into
shared hardware.** That is enough room to solve the 112-DSP problem without
touching the console's exact arithmetic laws.
