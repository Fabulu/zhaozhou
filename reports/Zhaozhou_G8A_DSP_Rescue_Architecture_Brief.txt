01. EXECUTIVE DECISION AND NON-OVERLAPPING TARGETS
===============================================================================

Do not attack the reciprocal first. The largest local DSP owner is the three
attribute-gradient lanes, which collectively use 24 physical DSP blocks. Their
multiplications are job-setup work, while the ongoing pixel walk uses addition.
This is unusually attractive arithmetic to share without serializing the actual
pixel walk. [R01,R02]

The selected first portfolio is:

  ATTR3: three existing 8-DSP attribute lanes -> three 3-DSP workers = -15
  BIL2:  four existing 3-DSP bilerp lanes -> four 2-DSP lanes        =  -4
                                                                    ---
  Primary structural objective: 49 -> 30 physical DSP blocks          -19

Then consider:

  UV2: two existing 3-DSP perspective products -> two 2-DSP products = -2
  Expanded structural objective: 49 -> 28 physical DSP blocks

Optional alternative:

  ATTR1 replaces ATTR3 with one 27-bit multiplier per attribute lane.
  Attribute contribution becomes 3 rather than 9 DSP blocks.
  That is six additional blocks below ATTR3, NOT another -21 below ATTR3.

  Portfolio                  ATTR   BILERP   PERSP   OTHER   TOTAL   DELTA
  Historical G8A timing2        24       12       6       7      49       0
  BIL2 alone                   24        8       6       7      45      -4
  ATTR3 alone                   9       12       6       7      34     -15
  ATTR3 + BIL2                  9        8       6       7      30     -19
  ATTR3 + BIL2 + UV2            9        8       4       7      28     -21
  ATTR1 alone                   3       12       6       7      28     -21
  ATTR1 + BIL2                  3        8       6       7      24     -25
  ATTR1 + BIL2 + UV2            3        8       4       7      22     -27

Every row except the historical row is a proposed structure, not a new receipt.
The unmodified remainder is a residual of the inspected hierarchy, not a promise
that composition will preserve an identical physical remainder under a new fit.

DECISION: Build toward ATTR3 + BIL2 first. Do not stall that useful endpoint on
ATTR1, on an unrelated signedness mode in a generic wrapper, or on UV2's optional
recombination timing. Investigate ATTR1 through a bounded implementation/model
only if it does not derail the primary endpoint.

No feature, arithmetic precision, camera count, fragment order, texture format,
filter lane count, clock target, frame obligation or legal program is removed.
If a proposal cannot preserve its required behavior/rate, it is not an accepted
implementation of this brief.

03. WHAT THE CURRENT 49 DSPS ACTUALLY COVER
===============================================================================

The native G8A timing2 result is 12,772 ALMs, 49 physical DSP blocks, 71 reported
RAM blocks and 84.95 MHz. The 100 MHz timing rule remains failed. It is a
three-plane connected subsystem characterization, not a final console fit. [R01]

The non-overlapping DSP cover used here is:

  u_tile.g_attr[0].u_attrgrad      8
  u_tile.g_attr[1].u_attrgrad      8
  u_tile.g_attr[2].u_attrgrad      8      attribute total 24

  u_texture_v3.g_bilerp[0]         3
  u_texture_v3.g_bilerp[1]         3
  u_texture_v3.g_bilerp[2]         3
  u_texture_v3.g_bilerp[3]         3      bilerp total 12

  u_texture_v3.u_persp            6      paired perspective total 6

  everything else in the 49       7      leave unchanged in this brief

The nested divider in each attrgrad uses zero DSP. Sharing those dividers would
not recover the 24 blocks and would create a separate throughput problem.
The two edgewalk DSPs are inside the residual seven, not a new charge to add.
Do not sum the inclusive texture parent on top of its bilerp/perspective children.

These counts identify where to act, but they do not establish exact attribution
of every internal multiplier expression to a physical mode. The new map must
reconcile the replacement's complete physical hierarchy, not assume that source
asterisks are physical DSP counts. [R01,R08,V01,V02]


04. ARITHMETIC INVARIANTS AND WIDTH RULES
===============================================================================

4.1 Terminology

  sN(x): interpret N raw bits as a two's-complement signed integer.
  uN(x): interpret N raw bits as unsigned.
  sxW:   sign-extend to W bits BEFORE arithmetic or shifting.
  zxW:   zero-extend to W bits BEFORE arithmetic or shifting.
  wrapN: retain low N bits and interpret them as signed.
  floor: mathematical floor, including negative inputs.

The numbers below describe the inspected ports, not informal typical gameplay
ranges. Do not reduce widths because the current test scene happens to be small.

4.2 Attribute law that must remain exact

Inputs to each lane:

  n0                  signed 96
  dNdx, dNdy          signed 72
  area2               unsigned 47; zero is a terminal error in the leaf
  global min_x        signed 12
  tile_x, tile_y      signed 12
  tile_x - min_x      signed 13, subtraction AFTER sign extension
  grad_x and q        signed 32
  tile offset         signed 45

With dx/dy sign-extended as necessary:

  base = wrap96(n0 + dx*min_x + dy*tile_y + floor(dx/2) + floor(dy/2))
  row_n = wrap96(base + dy*coverage_row)

  div_current(n,a) = sat_s32(floor((n + floor(a/2))/a)), for a > 0
  grad_x          = div_current(dx, area2)
  row_q           = div_current(row_n, area2)
  offset          = exact_s45(grad_x * (tile_x - min_x))
  seed            = wrap32(row_q + offset)
  pixel_q(c)      = wrap32(seed + c*grad_x)

Negative exact halves round toward positive infinity in div_current. The shifts
of negative dx/dy are arithmetic/floor halves, not truncation toward zero.
Saturation belongs to division. The global-to-tile seed and pixel walk wrap;
they must not start saturating because a shared arithmetic helper saturates.

Preserve the original sequence of numerical truncations. Intermediate exact
products fit well within 96 bits, but n0 plus those products can cross a 96-bit
rail for arbitrary legal port bit patterns. Addition modulo 2^96 is associative,
so the proposed serial base fold reproduces the current RTL's final low 96 bits.
Do not pass an unbounded Python or C++ sum to the divider and pretend that is
always equivalent to the RTL. Separately test the valid production ZRef domain.

4.3 Never redivide at the tile edge

The once-rounded grad_x is accumulated from GLOBAL min_x. A fresh division at the
tile's left edge changes results. Simple witness: area=3, dx=1, row numerator at
min_x=0, tile delta=2. Rounded grad_x is 0, so the stepped value is 0; a fresh
rounded division of 2/3 is 1. The existing omitted-min-X mutant protects this
boundary. [R02,R06]

4.4 No "zero means free" shortcut

Keep zero-area errors, saturated quotients, depth-zero perspective results and
all logical event counters. Constant/zero operands may simplify naturally in
Quartus, but they do not justify deleting complete runtime operand paths from
the selected legal domain or changing divide/product counts.


05. THE EXACT 27-BIT MULTIPLICATION BOUNDARY
===============================================================================

5.1 Required mathematical interface

A small local/common helper, proposed name zhao_mul27_exact, computes:

  a_i: signed 27
  b_i: signed 27
  p_o: signed 54 = a_i * b_i, exact and unrounded

The helper itself is combinational and stateless. Caller stages own clocks,
enables, reset, valid, tags and result holding. Do not add an internal ready
protocol or shared job queue. The mapping objective is one physical Cyclone V
DSP per live helper instance. Cyclone V explicitly supports a single 27x27 mode;
that is a device capability, not proof of this new helper's mapping. [V01,V02]

5.2 First implementation choice: simple exact inference, then inspect

An explicitly signed 27-by-27 expression assigned directly to a signed 54-bit
signal, with the local multstyle="dsp" preference, is a reasonable FIRST mapped
candidate. Do not put a 96-bit cast around the operands at this boundary. Keep
all extension/recombination outside it. Do not apply a broad DSP preference to
unrelated adders or the entire subsystem.

The documented multstyle attribute is a preference, not a guarantee. The source
index points to a Standard 18.1 guide for that distinction; the installed 17.0.2
compiler and its actual result decide the local mapping. [V03]

Only one bounded MapOnly discriminator is needed to establish whether the exact
used operand shapes and output bits map to the intended blocks. Observe actual
27-bit/native DSP instances and complete output use, with dynamically varied
inputs and observed results. Constant or unconnected inputs are not evidence.

5.3 If inference does not produce the required count

Use a native Cyclone V 27x27 implementation behind the same exact boundary.
Derive the full parameter/port set from the INSTALLED Quartus-17 declaration,
XML/IP generator and library setup. Do not copy the dual18 atom parameter set
and merely replace its mode string: the 27-bit mode's port configuration must
be checked independently. This document deliberately does not invent that recipe.

Before accepting a custom native atom, demonstrate:

  - exact selected mode and operand widths;
  - one physical DSP in the genuine map;
  - all required product bits remain live;
  - signed product behavior on the relevant full operand domain via the real
    vendor model or an equivalently approved technology-level proof;
  - the portable branch and custom atom cannot coexist accidentally.

If using a custom primitive, use an explicit exclusive synthesis/behavioral
selector, like the existing zhao_dual18_mul discipline. Do not let an unselected
portable fallback silently pass a native-mapping test. If simple inference is
selected instead, say so honestly; no custom-atom simulation claim is implied.

The geometry projection service is not a prerequisite for this tiny boundary.
Do not delay the DSP-rescue kernel on the larger shared-projector programme.


06. ATTR3: THREE DSPS PER ATTRIBUTE LANE
===============================================================================

6.1 Replace duplicated expressions with one local worker

The current lane has two large products in base_min_y0_c on job acceptance and
one grad_x*tile_delta product on gradient return. The row walk itself is add-only;
the small row-index offset is already a shift/add network. [R02]

Implement exactly ONE local 72x13 worker inside each attribute lane. Its three
physical 27-bit multipliers are shared between these opcodes:

  BASE_X: A = accepted dNdx; B = sign-extended accepted min_x.
  BASE_Y: A = accepted dNdy; B = sign-extended accepted tile_y.
  OFFSET: A = sign-extended captured grad_x; B = captured tile_delta.

The opcodes only route three known results inside one active job. They are not
new fragment identifiers, owner generations, externally programmable microcode,
or a new transaction-lifetime owner.

Keep three complete attribute/divider/walk lanes. This packet neither serializes
their output streams nor shares their dividers.

6.2 Exact decomposition

For signed72 A, use the RAW slices:

  L0 = u24(A[23:0])
  L1 = u24(A[47:24])
  H  = s24(A[71:48])

Then the exact signed integer identity is:

  A = L0 + (L1 << 24) + (H << 48)
  A*B = L0*B + ((L1*B) << 24) + ((H*B) << 48)

L0/L1 must be zero-extended to signed27; H and B must be sign-extended.
A negative A has an unsigned middle piece. Treating all three pieces as signed
is just as wrong as treating all three as unsigned.

Illustrative typed expressions (arithmetic skeleton, not tested production RTL):

  logic signed [26:0] a0, a1, ah, b27;
  logic signed [53:0] p0_full, p1_full, ph_full;

  assign a0  = $signed({3'b000, a72[23:0]});
  assign a1  = $signed({3'b000, a72[47:24]});
  assign ah  = $signed({{3{a72[71]}}, a72[71:48]});
  assign b27 = $signed({{14{b13[12]}}, b13});

  // Three separate exact helpers, each physically qualified as one DSP.
  // p0_full = a0*b27; p1_full = a1*b27; ph_full = ah*b27.

6.3 Sufficient pipeline widths

These are conservative exact widths, not a proposal to change the public ABI:

  low partials p0/p1: signed38
  high partial ph:   signed37
  s01 = p0 + p1<<24: signed62
  final product:     signed85

Before capturing shortened product registers, assert that all discarded helper
output bits are the corresponding sign extension. In the small OFFSET mode,
assert that product85[84:45] equals the sign extension of bit44 before capturing
signed45. The full signed32-by-signed13 product is exactly representable there.

To combine, SIGN EXTEND BEFORE SHIFTING:

  s01_62 = sx62(p0_38) + (sx62(p1_38) << 24)
  prod85 = sx85(s01_62) + (sx85(ph37_delayed) << 48)

A left shift in the original narrow operand width loses upper bits before a
later cast can recover them. Include a committed mutant for that error.

6.4 Concrete micro-pipeline

Feed helper operands from already registered job/gradient state and a small
registered phase selection. Define M as the rising edge of a micro-issue:

  Edge M:   capture the three partial products and operation tag.
  Edge M+1: capture s01_62; delay ph37 and the tag/valid.
  Edge M+2: capture prod85; publish result valid and its operation tag.
  Edge M+3: architectural consumer commits that result.

This worker has three edge intervals from micro-issue to consumer commit and
can accept a micro-operation each clock. It does not need an input payload bank
on top of the already registered job; adding one changes the calendar below.

Reset clears all stage-valid bits and operation-pending bits. Payload registers
need not be reset merely to hold zero when invalid. Reuse the project's existing
valid/reset discipline; never expose uninitialized payload as valid work.

6.5 Base accumulation without a second large sum tree

At job acceptance capture n0 in the base accumulator and the required dx/dy,
min_x, tile_y and tile_delta. Fold exactly one contribution per specified edge:

  floor(dx/2), floor(dy/2), BASE_X product, BASE_Y product.

Use wrapping96 addition after sign extension. Do not keep the original two
parallel wide multipliers or the original full base sum alongside the new path.
That would produce correctness but no DSP rescue.

The 96-bit accumulator is the eventual base_min_y0 register, not a separate
unbounded scratch queue. Product pipeline registers are private transient state.
There is no reason for this design to allocate any new M10K.

6.6 Expected physical structure

  Per lane: three DSPs for the one worker; zero DSPs in the retained divider.
  Three lanes: nine DSPs.
  Deleted: both original independent base product cones and the independent
           grad_x*tile_delta product cone in every selected candidate lane.

Map must show the deleted cones absent, not merely unconnected source variables.
The baseline old module may remain as an unselected oracle; it must not also be
instantiated in the measured candidate hierarchy.


07. ATTR3 FIXED SCHEDULE, INCLUDING FAST DIVIDER RESULTS
===============================================================================

7.1 The important correction to the informal proposal

The inspected divider's externally visible response latency after acceptance is:

  radix 2 ordinary nonsaturated divide: 100 clocks
  radix 4 ordinary nonsaturated divide:  51 clocks
  zero-area OR saturated divide:          2 clocks

The RTL performs 98 or 49 main iterations, but those are not the complete visible
latencies. The existing C++ test explicitly checks 100/51/2 and the corresponding
busy-clock delta. We leave that divider UNCHANGED. [R03,R07]

The earlier informal argument relied on ordinary division slack. This brief does
better: it supplies an ATTR3 schedule whose base and offset are ready before EVEN
THE EARLIEST first-row consumer needs them, assuming section 6's exact three-stage
micro-pipeline and no extra hidden operand stage. This is a proposed schedule
checked in an abstract model, not a claim that a new RTL implementation passed.

7.2 Edge convention

J is the rising edge accepting a new attribute job. A registered value produced
at edge J+n is available combinationally AFTER that edge; another always_ff
consumer reads it on the following edge unless explicitly sharing next-state
logic. Do not accidentally count same-edge nonblocking assignments as available
to another registered consumer.

Micro-result convention:

  micro-issue M -> partial capture M -> first combine M+1 -> final product M+2
  -> architectural result consumption/accumulation M+3.

The product inputs are read from existing registered job state before edge M.
There is no additional operand-capture stage hidden ahead of M.

7.3 Fixed base calendar

  Edge     Multiplier micro-issue       Base accumulator action
  J        none                        capture n0 and job state
  J+1      BASE_X                      base += arithmetic_half(dx)
  J+2      BASE_Y                      base += arithmetic_half(dy)
  J+3      none                        hold
  J+4      none                        base += completed BASE_X product
  J+5      OFFSET if fast grad ready   base += completed BASE_Y; base_ready=1

The simultaneous OFFSET issue and BASE_Y result consumption at J+5 are safe:
these are different pipeline stages, and OFFSET writes tile_offset rather than
the base accumulator when it later returns.

The base fold has four additions over separate edges rather than an input tree
of several wide additions. Every addition is modulo 2^96. A single adder can be
selected explicitly if inference would otherwise instantiate several adders.
Do not make a variable-width barrel-shift datapath: halves and product shifts
are fixed positions. Keep operand muxes limited to the named contributions.

7.4 Earliest gradient and coverage events

The unchanged high-level FSM accepts a gradient divide at J+1. On a fast result:

  J+3: divider response becomes visible.
  J+4: gradient result is consumed; grad_x and flags are captured.
  J+5: first coverage row can be accepted; OFFSET micro-issue uses captured grad.
  J+6: first row divide can be accepted, using the completed base from J+5.
  J+7: OFFSET final product becomes visible.
  J+8: tile_offset commits; earliest row response becomes visible.
  J+9: earliest row result is consumed and the pixel-walk seed commits.

Thus the three-DSP worker need not extend the shortest baseline first-row path.
It does NOT depend on pretending a two-cycle saturated divide takes 98 cycles.

For an ordinary radix-2 gradient:

  J+1:   gradient request accepted.
  J+101: response visible.
  J+102: gradient commits.
  J+103: first coverage accept and OFFSET micro-issue.
  J+104: row divide request accepted.
  J+106: offset commits; a fast row result can also become visible.
  J+107: earliest fast-row seed commits.

For radix 4, replace the ordinary gradient visible latency with 51; the relative
post-gradient calendar is identical. If coverage is delayed, both the original
and candidate row schedule are delayed, while completed base/offset data remain
held. Ordinary long-row division provides more slack, not a necessary premise.

7.5 The invariant to prove in RTL

Let G be the gradient response-consumption edge, C the coverage-accept edge and
D the row divide acceptance edge. For the first row:

  G >= J+4
  C >= G+1
  D >= C+1 >= J+6
  base_ready commits at J+5, so the base is available before D.

  offset_issue = G+1
  offset_commit = G+4
  earliest row result consumption = D + 2 + 1 >= G+5

The offset is therefore available before even the fastest row consumption.
This proof assumes the unchanged divider/FSM handshake and the stated kernel.
It is a useful implementation contract: do not casually add another multiplier
pipeline stage or move issue one edge later and retain the same claim.

7.6 Safe implementation guards

Do not feed an incomplete base to the divider or a stale offset to the seed
adder just to meet a cycle assertion. Guard row-divide valid with base readiness
and seed consumption with offset readiness if needed for fail-safe development.
Then assert that these guards NEVER introduce a stall in the qualified ATTR3
calendar. If they do, the implementation failed the zero-added-latency objective.
Do not weaken the assertion to hide the failure.

A changed current divider, changed start protocol, or an additional timing stage
requires a new calendar. Keep the arithmetic candidate; report the changed
latency and prove its actual workload before accepting it. Do not silently amend
an existing exact-latency test for the untouched divider.

7.7 No hidden full-frame claim

This schedule preserves a local baseline under its assumptions. It does not
certify that the old three-plane G8A path itself sustains final game throughput.
It retains the divider's current row cost. Later full-plane/terrain composition
still needs its own real workload proof. Passing this rescue does not turn the
16x16 characterization profile into the complete cel-shaded game.


08. ATTRIBUTE STATE, HANDSHAKE, CANCELLATION AND QUIET
===============================================================================

8.1 Suggested control implementation

Retain the high-level job/gradient/coverage/row/walk FSM and its public ports.
Add only the bounded local state needed to launch and recognize:

  BASE_X issued/completed
  BASE_Y issued/completed
  OFFSET issued/completed
  multiplier pipeline valids and a tiny internal operation tag
  base preparation phase and base_ready

Reuse existing job operands and grad_x/tile_delta storage where possible. Do not
store another full 1,157-bit triangle record or another per-fragment context.
There is exactly one active attribute job per lane, as in the inspected design.
A small phase counter or explicit phase bits are preferable to a generic queue.

Priority is deterministic: issue BASE_X at J+1, BASE_Y at J+2, and OFFSET once
captured grad_x is available. In the inspected divider the earliest OFFSET issue
is J+5, so the base issues cannot conflict with it. A duplicate-issue assertion
must fire if a control error violates that property.

8.2 Fixed local result sinks

The worker is a fixed pipeline with always-available local destinations:

  BASE_X/BASE_Y -> base accumulator on their scheduled result edges.
  OFFSET        -> signed45 tile_offset register.

Output backpressure on the pixel stream must not freeze this setup worker, replay
a product, duplicate an accumulator addition or change a valid output's data.
Because BASE_X and BASE_Y were issued on different clocks, they never contend for
the one base write port. OFFSET has a different destination. No local response
FIFO is necessary for this fixed schedule.

If an implementation introduces a variable-latency helper or cannot keep these
sinks available, it no longer matches this architecture. Design and prove the
new buffering explicitly rather than treating lost results as harmless stalls.

8.3 Preserve public handshake and exact outputs

Under identical legal input valid/data and output-ready traces, ATTR3's target is
cycle-by-cycle equivalence of:

  job_ready, cov_ready, q_valid;
  q value, row, column, last, saturated and error whenever valid;
  every held output under stall;
  idle/quiet observability;
  accepted coverage/pixel/divide/saturation/error event counts.

Keep the current wrapping pixel walk and all uncovered-column progression.
A zero coverage mask still follows the current FSM/counter semantics; do not
create a new "empty job" fast path without a separate contract and proof.

ATTR3 does not change the divider implementation, so its response-visible and
busy-counter tests must remain exactly as they were. Whole-lane cycle comparisons
must be added, not inferred from matching final pixels.

8.4 Error and cancellation behavior

Preserve current errors as values/events: gradient and row saturation flags are
combined as before; zero-area is a terminal divider error; invalid interpolated
invw is handled by the existing parent abort law, not by silent truncation.

The leaf does not gain a new asynchronous cancel or reset. The parent already
cancels/sinks unadmitted candidates and drains admitted work. Let the bounded
micro-operations finish and be included in local empty/idle checks. A recoverable
frame fault must not clear multiplier valid bits in a way that leaves the parent
waiting for a promised result forever. [R06,R09,R12]

On physical reset, clear all new valids and pending obligations. A result from a
pre-reset operation must never update a post-reset job. Test reset at every
micro-pipeline phase, not only when the old lane was idle.

8.5 Quiet and ownership

A lane cannot advertise idle if a setup operation or result remains live, even
if the high-level FSM is unexpectedly in IDLE. Healthy operation should have no
micro-work remaining by final pixel retirement. Assert that as a structural
invariant and expose no contradictory empty indication.

Do not create a second V3 owner, second raster continuation queue, second frame
sequence generator or new ownership registry. Internal X/Y/OFFSET tags are not
fragment identities. Existing row/column/last cross-lane identity checks, fault
priority, sink accounting, and one-owner guarantees remain mandatory.


09. ATTR1: ONE DSP PER ATTRIBUTE LANE, OPTIONAL SECOND ENDPOINT
===============================================================================

9.1 Purpose and relationship to ATTR3

ATTR1 is a more aggressive backend for the SAME numerical lane. It replaces
ATTR3's three simultaneous partial multipliers with one signed27x27 multiplier.
There are still three independent lanes and three unchanged dividers.

Attribute DSP target: 24 -> 3. Relative to accepted ATTR3, the incremental target
is only 9 -> 3, or six additional DSPs. Never double-count these alternatives.

Its resource attraction is real, but it can add latency to short-gradient jobs.
Do not select it automatically just because its map has the smallest DSP number.

9.2 Partial-product schedule

For each BASE operation use the same three-piece identity as ATTR3, but issue
one partial per clock. Keep the multiplier result in a register before adding
it to a wrapping signed85 accumulator. Avoid a multiplier-plus-wide-adder path
on one edge. The multiplier can accept one partial each clock; the accumulator
consumes the preceding registered product each clock.

For OFFSET only, exploit the actual signed32 operand without losing semantics:

  G = u24(G[23:0]) + (s8(G[31:24]) << 24)
  G*delta = low24*delta + ((signed_high8*delta) << 24)

The second OFFSET piece is SIGNED8, not the unsigned middle24 used in BASE.
This is an opcode-dependent input extension into the same physical multiplier,
not another multiplier. It needs two partial operations rather than three.

9.3 Explicit calendar, job acceptance J=0

  Edge   Issue partial     Accumulator/architectural action
  J      none              base := n0; capture job
  J+1    BASE_X low        base += floor(dx/2)
  J+2    BASE_X middle     consume X low; base += floor(dy/2)
  J+3    BASE_X high       accumulate X middle
  J+4    BASE_Y low        accumulate X high; X complete visible
  J+5    BASE_Y middle     base += X; initialize accumulator with Y low
  J+6    BASE_Y high       accumulate Y middle
  J+7    OFFSET low*       accumulate Y high; Y complete visible
  J+8    OFFSET high*      base += Y; consume OFFSET low
  J+9    none*             accumulate OFFSET high; OFFSET complete visible
  J+10   none*             commit tile_offset

*This earliest OFFSET schedule applies when the fast gradient committed at J+4.
For a later gradient commit G, issue OFFSET at G+1 and G+2; commit at G+4.
In general, first OFFSET issue is max(J+7, G+1).

The base accumulator and partial-product accumulator are different registers
with different roles. Do not accidentally overwrite the completed base with the
later offset accumulation; every covered row still needs that base.

The completed-partial sum can be consumed on the edge that initializes the next
operation's accumulator, using the pre-edge completed value. Maintain exact
operation tags/valids across that edge. A second 85-bit result FIFO is not required
if this local always-ready consumption protocol is implemented as specified.

9.4 Latency consequence

ATTR1's base commits at J+8 instead of J+5. The first row divide therefore cannot
accept before J+9. With a fast gradient the old row divide could accept at J+6;
under the stated schedule, this can introduce three startup clocks.

In the supplied ABSTRACT first-row calendars:

  ATTR3 maximum added first-seed clocks: 0
  ATTR1 maximum added first-seed clocks: 3

Ordinary radix-2/radix-4 gradient division hides the BASE sequence. Delayed first
coverage can also hide it. The OFFSET sequence still fits the earliest row-result
window under the stated stages. None of this is an executed RTL timing claim.

The three-clock figure is not an unconditional end-to-end frame upper bound:
closed-loop stalls and arbitration can amplify or mask a local phase shift.
Do not multiply it by a guessed triangle count and call that a workload proof.
Measure real tile-job counts (including the same triangle visiting multiple
tiles), short-gradient frequency, coverage distribution and the connected frame.

9.5 Qualification required for selection

ATTR1 may be selected only if:

  - values and logical counters match the current law;
  - every held interface remains stable and all work drains under fair readiness;
  - the measured connected workload meets its required deadlines with reserve;
  - any exact latency/acceptance contract change has explicit authorization;
  - extra mux/control/accumulator cost is acceptable on the ALM axis;
  - the selected map actually has one DSP per attribute worker;
  - it beats ATTR3 on a trade the complete machine needs.

If those questions remain open, retain ATTR3 and do not block BIL2 or roadmap
progress. A passing arithmetic differential alone does not authorize slower
production acceptance. A 30-DSP primary result is already worth landing.


10. BIL2: TWO DSPS PER BILERP LANE WITHOUT FEWER FILTER LANES
===============================================================================

10.1 Precisely what to pack

Keep the four existing bilerp channel workers. In EACH worker, replace only the
two horizontal products in the B0-to-B1 stage:

  du0 = s9(t10 - t00)
  du1 = s9(t11 - t01)
  fu  = u8(fraction_u)
  pu0 = du0 * fu
  pu1 = du1 * fu

The products are simultaneous, use the same b1_ready decision and b0_valid token,
and produce separate results. They are ideal partners for the existing stateless
zhao_dual18_mul boundary. The later vertical product remains unchanged in the
next pipeline stage. [R04,R08]

10.2 Use all-signed primitive settings for this consumer

The fractions are nonnegative, so sign-extend the differences and ZERO-extend
fractions, then use signed multiplication on BOTH inputs of BOTH lanes:

  AX_SIGNED=1, AY_SIGNED=1, BX_SIGNED=1, BY_SIGNED=1

  ax = {{9{du0[8]}}, du0[8:0]}
  ay = {10'b0, fu[7:0]}
  bx = {{9{du1[8]}}, du1[8:0]}
  by = {10'b0, fu[7:0]}

The exact typed reconstruction is:

  pu0_s18 = s18(resulta[17:0])
  pu1_s18 = s18(resultb[17:0])

Assert each 36-bit result's upper 18 bits are sign extension of bit17 before
that capture. Reachable differences are -255..255 and fractions 0..255, so the
products are well inside signed18. Do not treat an unsigned fraction with its
bit7 set as a negative signed8 operand.

This all-signed configuration avoids depending on a mixed-signedness mode for
this particular consumer. It does NOT let an unsigned-only primitive calibration
stand in for signed functional proof. Check the exact all-signed tuple used here.

10.3 Preserve the existing filter arithmetic

Keep:

  a = (t00 << 8) + pu0
  b = (t01 << 8) + pu1
  dv = b - a
  pv = dv * fv
  sum = (a << 8) + pv
  out = (sum + 32768) >> 16

Keep the original sufficient signed intermediate widths, full U intermediates,
and single final rounding. Do not quantize a/b to bytes early. An exact bilerp
is not the same as independently rounding two horizontal lerps and then lerping
them vertically. The supplied tests include a counterexample.

10.4 Preserve stage timing and control exactly

The current three elastic stages are:

  B0: texels, fractions, token, channel
  B1: exact horizontal intermediates, V fraction, token, channel
  B2: held output byte, token, channel

Ready propagates from B2 through B1 to B0. The primitive is combinational; the
caller retains the existing stage registers and enables. There is no new valid,
ready, latency, capacity, output reordering, or reset policy.

  r2 = !b2_valid || out_ready
  r1 = !b1_valid || r2
  r0 = !b0_valid || r1

The acceptance trace and output trace should therefore match cycle for cycle,
including arbitrary stalls, full occupancy, back-to-back jobs and resets. Match
jobs_o and occupancy_o as well as the final byte. On a held B1/B2 transaction,
do not update its paired products, token, channel or fraction independently.

10.5 Expected physical result and forbidden shortcuts

Per lane: one physical dual18 block for the two horizontal products, plus the
existing vertical-product block. Four lanes: 8 instead of 12 DSPs.

Do not:

  - reduce four filter lanes to one and claim this proposal's throughput;
  - pair horizontal and vertical products across different stallable stages;
  - pair unrelated workers with different stage enables;
  - accept a DSP mode label without proving both result routes are live;
  - implement a sum-of-products primitive when two independent outputs are needed;
  - synthesize both behavioral and vendor branches;
  - replace the primitive with a new bespoke packing framework.

The existing wrapper and route-evidence work are assets to reuse. A failed generic
unused signedness combination need not block a proven consumer tuple, but any
failure in the tuple/width/domain actually used by BIL2 is a real HOLD.


11. UV2: TWO DSPS PER PERSPECTIVE COORDINATE
===============================================================================

11.1 Exact product structure

Current u_persp calculates signed32 numerator times unsigned24 reciprocal
mantissa for U and V in parallel. It then rounds, shifts, saturates and routes the
paired result through its existing credit/FIFO/output machinery. The inspected
physical contribution is six DSP blocks. [R01,R05]

For either signed32 N and unsigned24 M:

  L = u26(N[25:0])
  H = s6(N[31:26])
  N = L + (H << 26)
  N*M = L*M + ((H*M) << 26)

Both products fit one signed27x27 boundary after these exact extensions:

  low27  = {1'b0, N[25:0]}          // zero-extended, then interpreted signed
  high27 = {{21{N[31]}}, N[31:26]}  // sign-extended
  mant27 = {3'b000, M[23:0]}       // nonnegative signed27

There are TWO helpers for U and TWO for V, not one helper time-shared between
coordinates. The paired acceptance-rate target remains one pair per clock.

11.2 Product and storage widths

  L*M is unsigned50.
  H*M is signed30.
  Recombined product is signed56; sign-extend into the original signed64 path.

When narrowing a helper's full signed54 output, assert upper-zero bits on the
low product and upper-sign bits on the high product. Do not store the signed6
high chunk as unsigned6. N=-1 is a simple decisive falsifier.

Do not first sign-extend M to signed24: mantissa bit23 is a value bit, not a sign.
Do not round either partial, discard fractional low bits, or saturate before
recombining the exact full product.

11.3 Preferred same-latency placement

Keep P0 acceptance unchanged. Replace P1's stored full U/V products with the two
partial products per coordinate. Do NOT store both the original full product and
all the partials. P2 computes the same rounded sum from the partials; P3 performs
the existing rescale/saturation preparation, and the FIFO/output path stays in
place.

Current shape:

  P0 operands -> P1 full product -> P2 product + rounding -> P3 rescaled value

Candidate shape:

  P0 operands -> P1 low/high partials -> P2 reconstructed product + rounding
              -> P3 rescaled value

This preserves registered-stage count. It moves an extra recombination addition
into P2; that path must not become the next timing problem.

11.4 Carry-save recombination option

Use a local 64-bit three-operand carry-save reduction followed by one carry-
propagate addition if the straightforward two-adder expression creates a long
chain. Let all x/y/z below be raw64 bit vectors:

  x = zx64(low50)
  y = sx64(high30) << 26
  z = the ORIGINAL raw64 rounding vector

  s = x XOR y XOR z
  c = ((x AND y) OR (x AND z) OR (y AND z)) << 1
  rounded_sum = wrap64(s + c)

The identity is exact modulo 2^64. No new numerical law or intermediate rounding
is introduced. Keep the existing signed interpretation for the later arithmetic
right shift. This is a small local expression, not a general-purpose arithmetic
service.

The required final product itself fits signed56, but the old 64-bit rounding and
shift semantics must be preserved separately. Do not assume all six-bit k codes
are legal normalization exponents and quietly simplify their behavior.

11.5 Preserve the actual shift and edge cases

The inspected source uses six-bit sh = 32-k, a 64-bit rounding bit shifted by
sh-1, and then a signed64 arithmetic shift. Keep those widths and operations
unchanged. In particular, sh=0/high k bit patterns can have surprising raw-bit
behavior. This brief does not authorize correcting that behavior while optimizing
multiplication. The supplied Python model mirrors six-bit subtraction and 64-bit
wrap, but compiled RTL tests must be the final authority on HDL semantics.

Check all 64 k encodings at the leaf unless the current authoritative port
contract explicitly rejects some. Independently include the actual RCP-produced
k/mantissa domain in the connected test. Do not exclude awkward values merely
because a typical camera does not generate them.

Keep depth_zero exactly: output U/V zero and saturation false, with the same
logical zero-product counter semantics. Logical products_o still counts two
coordinate products per nonzero-depth pair; it does not become four because the
implementation now uses four partial products.

11.6 Credit/FIFO preservation

Keep the existing NTOK/TAGW parameters, CAP=NTOK+1, admission ownership count,
terminal FIFO, read/write pointers, tag, sat and depth-zero pairing. This packet
must not shrink capacity or introduce a shared scalar multiplier bottleneck.

Preferred acceptance criterion: cycle-exact valid/ready/output/counter parity
against the unchanged paired implementation at the same parameters.

If a physical timing result forces an extra stage, treat it as an explicit
alternative requiring a complete in-flight/credit, queue-capacity, latency and
connected-throughput proof. An additional pipeline register is not permission
to add another admission credit or another output queue. Do not weaken tests to
make an unplanned stage disappear from the contract.

UV2 is optional. If it adds unacceptable ALMs or timing risk, land ATTR3+BIL2 at
its qualified target and defer the extra two DSPs.


12. TIMING AND ALM CONTAINMENT
===============================================================================

12.1 DSP count alone is not success

The owner is trying to fit a complete console, not minimize one column while
making another impossible. Every physical comparison must report, at the same
boundary and parameters:

  physical DSP blocks;
  ALMs using the SAME metric as the baseline receipt;
  registers;
  reported RAM blocks plus actual M10K/MLAB inference witnesses where available;
  logical memory bits;
  worst required-clock setup and hold slack, Fmax, and critical path families;
  functional workload/throughput and drain behavior.

Do not compare combinational ALUTs with ALMs, or an entity's self number with a
parent's inclusive number. Do not translate fewer declared flops directly into
fewer fitted ALMs. Explicit reconstruction may trade DSPs for fabric adders.

12.2 Initial area objective

Default objective: no net ALM increase at the matched G8A boundary. This is a
proposed decision objective for this packet, not a newly ratified console quota.
There is no automatic authorization in this brief to spend hundreds or thousands
of extra ALMs for the smallest theoretical DSP endpoint.

If a useful DSP reduction has a measured positive ALM delta, report that exact
trade and its physical owner, then compare the available alternatives. For
example, ATTR3+BIL2 may be preferable to adding UV2 if UV2's last two DSPs require
an unattractive carry/queue expansion. Do not hide the trade behind a much older
or differently parameterized ALM baseline.

12.3 Expected sources of cost, not numerical estimates

ATTR3 adds/changes:

  - one bounded operand selector into each local worker;
  - partial-product and recombination pipeline registers;
  - a tiny phase/tag/valid controller;
  - a serial base fold and fixed-position recombination adders.

It removes:

  - multiple independently instantiated wide multiplication cones;
  - the original independent offset multiplication;
  - the original parallel base preparation arrangement, if it is truly replaced.

ATTR1 trades three simultaneous partial multipliers for one and an accumulator/
selector. UV2 may store more partial-result bits than the old product stage and
needs a local recombination network. BIL2 mostly reuses existing stage state,
but packing can change placement and routing. Net area is not knowable from
these lists alone. Do not put a predicted ALM saving into an accepted ledger.

12.4 Timing disciplines

ATTR3's product output is registered before recombination. Its two combine
stages each have one principal carry-propagate operation. Its base fold adds one
contribution per edge. Do not collapse that back into a multiplier plus two or
three wide additions on a single critical edge for the sake of a prettier FSM.

If the 96-bit fold or 85-bit reconstruction itself cannot meet 100 MHz, derive
a bounded split-carry implementation or another local schedule. Any extra stage
must reenter section 7's fast-path proof. Do not trade a known DSP problem for
another indefinite timing-closure loop without an explicit alternative endpoint.

BIL2 deliberately uses the existing stage/enable boundaries. UV2 keeps the
original stage count with a three-input reduction as needed. The native DSP's
published standalone performance is NOT a timing guarantee for these surrounding
muxes, wide adders, register placement or complete G8A subsystem. [V02]

12.5 Same clock and no timing exceptions as a "rescue"

Keep the 100 MHz product constraint and the same applicable clock/corner rules.
Do not introduce a faster private DSP clock, multicycle constraints on paths that
still transfer every clock, false paths over real control, or a lower frequency
to make the resource result look qualified. No new CDC is needed here.

A completed fit can provide useful DSP/area evidence while its overall timing
is still red. Label it accordingly. It cannot promote Packet F or production
adoption. If timing3 is still red, retain that known failure; do not blame or
credit this packet for unchanged failures without reading actual paths.

19. ACCEPTANCE, ROLLBACK AND STOP CONDITIONS
===============================================================================

19.1 Evidence vocabulary

  Architected: this document specifies a concrete candidate.
  Mathematically checked: host identities/model cases passed.
  RTL verified: compiled RTL matches the appropriate independent oracle/tests.
  Mapped: selected technology structure was genuinely produced by Quartus.
  Fitted: complete placement/resource/timing result exists for the exact closure.
  Adopted: selected functional production composition uses it and old hardware
           is absent, after the existing adoption gate.

Do not promote a state by changing a label. The host checks supplied with this
brief establish only the second state for the particular mathematics/models.

19.2 Primary success conditions

The primary portfolio succeeds when:

  - current arithmetic law, legal input domain and features remain unchanged;
  - ATTR3 and BIL2 retain their qualified public service/holding behavior;
  - expected local physical DSP structure is demonstrated;
  - the complete selected G8A receipt actually approaches/achieves the proposed
    30-DSP endpoint, with no old/new double instantiation or pruned obligations;
  - ALM/RAM/timing trade is acceptable and explicitly reported;
  - the unchanged 100 MHz gate passes before any timing-qualified adoption claim;
  - applicable connected workload, ownership and reset/fault gates pass;
  - production adoption follows the existing composition rules, not this table.

A measured 31/32 instead of 30 is not "30 because the arithmetic says so." Read
where the extra blocks landed and decide whether a bounded repair changes the
outcome. Conversely, a lower count caused by pruned legal work is a failure.

19.3 Immediate rejection conditions

Reject or roll back the affected candidate if it:

  - changes a negative-half, signed extension, wrap, saturation or final-round law;
  - loses/misassociates a tag, row, column, status, owner handle or result;
  - gates valid with its own ready to conceal a lost operation;
  - signals idle with a live micro-operation or deletes required fault handling;
  - reduces a filter lane count or serializes paired U/V throughput without ruling;
  - changes required clock/frame/precision/camera/capability limits;
  - keeps the old arithmetic in addition to the candidate;
  - gets smaller only by choosing a weaker characterization profile;
  - adds unbounded queues/framework control or large unexplained ALM growth;
  - claims native behavior from only a portable-backend test;
  - claims fitted ALM/Fmax from a MapOnly estimate;
  - uses a new identical-rerun loop rather than a named physical diagnosis.

19.4 Safe rollback

Keep baseline source and tests recoverable by commit and keep candidate selection
explicit. Revert the candidate's leaf selection, helper/source closure, interface
or parameter additions, ownership expectations and generated artifacts together.
Do not revert independent accepted timing3 fixes or unrelated Packet-H work.

If BIL2's primitive mode is blocked, retain the original bilerp lanes while
continuing ATTR3. If UV2 is unattractive, leave u_persp unchanged. If ATTR1 misses
its schedule, use ATTR3. These separable decisions are why the primary portfolio
is not dependent on the most aggressive row in the table.

Do not destroy a valid baseline bitstream or load a new image merely to prove
an arithmetic idea. Board loading is outside this brief's scope.


20. WHAT THESE CHANGES DO NOT PROVE
===============================================================================

20.1 Not a final console cost

G8A is a narrower three-plane raster/texture characterization. The Packet-D
record explicitly keeps RGB/A flat and leaves additional Gouraud and world-X/W,
world-Z/W planes for later work. It cannot establish a complete final attribute,
lighting, fog/emission or AUX resource bill. [R06]

The useful reusable lesson is that a future attribute lane need not automatically
acquire another permanent 8-DSP setup arrangement. Additional planes still cost
state, dividers, transport, arithmetic service and workload. Do not label them
free, and do not extend the 30/28/22 table to a different plane count silently.

20.2 Not universal division acceleration

The current long division remains unchanged. This proposal spends less hardware
on work surrounding it. It does not prove an arbitrary full scene can afford all
of those row divisions, or that final scene throughput is one shaded pixel every
clock merely because the inner walk can offer at that rate.

20.3 Not ALM savings by definition

DSP packing/sharing may free scarce DSPs while costing additional ALMs. The goal
is a useful complete resource trade. Do not create another exaggerated ALM
expectation out of a sound DSP target.

20.4 Not a product concession

Unlike a fallback machine that drops split-screen, programmability or lighting,
these candidates retain the current mathematical and functional promises.
If a rate concession becomes necessary for ATTR1 or a new UV stage, surface it;
that variant then needs an explicit product/scheduling decision, not a hidden
optimization label.

20.5 Not a request to perfect every mathematical engine before proceeding

The owner wants continued concrete optimization and progress into the existing
large ALM targets. Land the useful qualified local endpoint and continue the
roadmap. A theoretical 22-DSP G8A should not keep a practical 30-DSP replacement
unpublished while six more speculative schedules are designed.
