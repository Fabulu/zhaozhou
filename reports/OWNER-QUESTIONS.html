The questions blocking the console

Every decision that is yours and not mine, in one place. Zhaozhou, 2026‑08‑31.

It is not your fault, and the framing is worth correcting. The console is not stalled on effort or on anyone's mistake. The audit in reports/CONSOLE_REMAINING.md found that zero of 92 blocks can be built today — not because they are hard, but because the laws they would have to obey have never been written down. Ten of them are blocked by your own standing instruction not to invent game behaviour, which was the right instruction: a block built on invented laws ratifies those laws by existing. One contract already says so in its own words: “a small block built on four invented laws is worse than no block, because the inventions become ratified by being built.”

You do not need to answer all of this. Question 1 alone unblocks the renderer's last piece, and questions 2–4 are each worth a whole subsystem.

1Depth profile selection fastest win

Everything else about depth is now built, generated and proved. This one field is all that is missing, and it blocks GEOM.PROJECT's attribute carry — the renderer's final step‑6 piece.

How does a view say which depth profile it wants?

The three profiles exist, are generated by fixgen, and are proved monotonic over 200,000 consecutive near‑plane units:

0  WORLD_LONG       1.0 m .. 16384 m    scale 2^40    far floor 1024
1  WORLD_STANDARD   0.5 m ..  8192 m    scale 2^39    far floor 1024
2  CLOSE           0.25 m ..  2048 m    scale 2^38    far floor 2048

Nothing selects one at runtime. Your earlier ruling permitted either route and left the audit to decide; the audit does not settle it because both work.

  • (a) Two reserved bits of SetView.flags — free, no new command, but spends 2 of a small reserved pool forever.
  • (b) A small additive view‑depth command — costs a command opcode, but leaves room to add a fourth profile or custom near/far later.

Why it must be you: it is a permanent command ABI. Once a shipped capture contains it, it cannot be changed without breaking replay.

3Three numbers

Each of these is a real capacity decision with a cost attached. I can give you the tradeoff; I should not pick the value.

Binner arena capacity costs memory

The renderer today holds 128 triangles and 1,024 tile references. Those are test capacities. An army needs roughly 150× the triangles and 25× the references.

This is why the fit reporting “3 % block memory” is a number to be suspicious of rather than pleased by — it measures the placeholder, not the game.

The question: what is the largest battle that must never drop a triangle? An answer like “200 creatures plus terrain plus one level‑9 spell” is enough; I can convert that into capacity and tell you what it costs in M10K.

What does 276,480 count?

The per‑pixel budget names this number and does not say whether it counts pixels or fragments (a pixel can be shaded several times when things overlap).

It decides whether two per‑pixel blocks need replicating at all — i.e. whether a chunk of silicon gets built twice.

Cel‑material fog ordering visual

The general law mixes fog into vertex colour before rasterisation. For the toon ramp that is wrong: distant fog gets quantised into hard bands.

The right order for cel is interpolate unfogged light → toon → modulate texture → apply fog, which is cheap now that ATTRSTEP exists. But it contradicts the general per‑vertex fog law, so it has to be a deliberate amendment rather than something I quietly do.

Why it must be you: it changes how the world looks at distance, and it splits one law into two.

6Not questions — just so the list is honest

Six blocks wait on a physical board, not on you. Nothing unblocks them but hardware.

Five FIELD.SEQ.* entries — EARTH, WARP, FLOW, FORMATION, STAMP — are Field IR programs, not datapaths. The engine they run on is built and heavily optimised. Authoring one is closer to content than to hardware, and can happen any time.

Twelve software entries sit behind hardware by your own standing direction, which still looks right.

If you only answer one thing: question 1. It is a single choice between two options and it closes the renderer's last specified gap.

If you answer three: add the binner capacity (“what is the biggest battle?”) and the compositor ordering. Those two are each worth a subsystem.
