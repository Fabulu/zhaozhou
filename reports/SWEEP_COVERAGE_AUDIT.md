## Why this is a real gap and not bookkeeping

A green test lane says the block passes its tests. It does not say the tests
would **notice** if the block were wrong. The only evidence for that second
claim is a mutation sweep, and this project has already been bitten by the
difference more than once:

* TERRAIN.PATCH (2026-08-27) had 1,409 passing checks and a sweep found, on its
  first run, that **nothing in the file had ever read `fld_add_ready_o`** — so
  an intake that stalled instead of rejecting was invisible, in direct
  contradiction of the law the contract leads with.
* FIELD v2's ALU (2026-08-27) had every MAD test passing while the addend could
  have been read live instead of carried across a pipeline stage, because every
  existing test happened to put the same register behind it.
* MEM.VRAM.ARBITER once carried RTL_VERIFIED citing a formal property whose
  assertions were vacuous (recorded in `tools/ledger/src/rules.ts`).

In every case the tests were green and the coverage was imaginary.

### The two failures that were in the SWEEP MACHINERY, not the RTL

Both were found late on 2026-08-27, and both made a run that tested less than
it claimed look like a clean pass. They are recorded here because this
document exists to say how much the evidence is worth.

**A discarded mutant was being counted as accounted for.** Five field-family
drivers added the discard count into `accounted`, so the cross-check that
exists to catch a short run passed; the discard block then printed a NOTE and
fell through to `SWEEP OK` with exit 0. The patch accumulator's sweep printed
that after scoring 8 of 15 -- and, on the run before, 6 of 15. The seventeen
older drivers exclude discards from `accounted`, which is exactly what made
the RASTER.EARLYZ re-score fail with `attempted=16 accounted=12` instead of
quietly scoring 12 of 16. All five now exit 13 on an unscored mutant
(9ddf067).

**Every discard had the same cause: one shared build directory.** Three
sessions were building in `build/` at once, so targets would not link and
mutants were dropped. Re-run in isolation, the patch accumulator caught all
15 and RASTER.EARLYZ all 16 -- so those were catches lost to contention, not
survivors. Sweeps now configure their own build tree, with the generator and
compiler pinned so a stray CMakePresets cannot redirect them.

The lesson generalises past this tree: **a guard that can be satisfied by
work not being done is not a guard.** Both failures had that shape.

## Two entries worth calling out by name

**`zhao_debug_frameblit`.** DEBUG.FRAMEBLIT is recorded as CLOSED in
`reports/REMAINING_BLOCKERS.md` — RTL_VERIFIED on the composed path, with a
directed lane, a lint lane and a formal safety proof, all green. It has never
been mutated. Everything said about it is true and none of it is evidence that
its tests have teeth.

**`zhao_vertex_arena`.** GEOM.WCACHE was proved unboundedly this morning
(`abc pdr`, inductive, plus covers for non-vacuity). The FORMAL side is now the
strongest evidence in the tree. Its **simulation** lane has still never been
mutated, and the two ask different questions: the proof says the properties
hold, the sweep would say whether the directed test would notice a change the
properties do not constrain.

## What this does not say

It does not say these 38 blocks are wrong. Nothing here found a defect. It says
the confidence attached to them rests on a claim nobody has tested, and it
names the 38 places where that is true so the gap can be closed deliberately
rather than discovered.

It also does not say all 38 are equal work. A sweep costs roughly a morning:
the mutant table is the design work, and the driver and preflight are inherited
almost verbatim (see `tools/sweep_terrain_patch.sh`, derived in an afternoon
from the normals sweep with its provenance stripped rather than renamed).

Order to work them, highest value first: blocks that are *declared closed*
(FRAMEBLIT, WCACHE), then blocks on the hot path everything else depends on
(the raster six, `zhao_cmd_dma`), then the rest.
