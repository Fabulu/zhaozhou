B. Contracts are written — blocked on GAME BEHAVIOUR, not on specification

Nine blocks have substantially complete contracts (150–223 lines each: packet layouts, Q formats, counters, test plans). They are blocked by your standing direction:

"Do NOT invent game behaviour for the particle-simulation, compositor or 2D blocks."

PART.STATE, PART.UPDATE, PART.COLLIDE, PART.SPAWN, PART.LADDER, TWOD.PLANE, TWOD.SPRITE, POST.GATHER, POST.COMPOSITE

What is needed is a design decision, not a contract. Specifically:

  1. What does a particle do? Forces, lifetime, collision response against terrain and creatures, and what happens at the population ceiling — does it refuse to spawn, or evict the oldest?
  2. What is the 2D plane for? HUD only, or gameplay elements? Does it depth-test against the 3D scene or composite strictly on top?
  3. What does the compositor combine, in what order, and does anything read back the framebuffer?

Answer those three and all nine unblock together.

D. THE ACTUAL OPEN QUESTIONS — these are what I need from you

D1. Depth quantisation: wmin, wmax, scale

Source: reports/OPEN-SPEC-DEPTH-QUANTISATION.md. Blocks GEOM.PROJECT's attribute carry — the renderer's last step-6 piece.

These three numbers are the depth range of the console. Needed:

  1. wmin — fx16 view-space forward distance. The near plane.
  2. wmax — fx16. The far plane. Should be at least the map diagonal if distant terrain is to depth-test correctly.
  3. scale and the shift rescale uses, so that w == wmin gives exactly 0xFFFFFF and w == wmax gives a sensible non-zero floor.

The third is constrained by the first two, so in practice this is two numbers and an arithmetic check.

D2. The kMesh budget — how many creatures at full mesh at once?

Source: reports/BINNER_CAPACITY_FOR_8KM_MAPS.md and my addendum 2026-09-02.

This one is now much cheaper than the old report says. That report concluded a frame-resident binner arena "cannot be built" (49% of the device's M10K for one army). That arithmetic assumed every creature at full mesh. The shipped LOD ladder makes that impossible — kSplat and kGlint emit a billboard quad, two triangles, not ninety-six. Measured through the shipped zref::Binner, 256 creatures:

  all kMesh (the old assumption)   24,576 tris   30,609 refs
  8 mesh / 24 micro / 96 splat / 128 glint    1,792 tris    2,259 refs
  16 / 32 / 96 / 112                          2,720 tris    3,426 refs

which makes a frame-resident arena 16–21% of M10K, not 49%. So a bigger constant will do — provided the governor bounds how many creatures sit at kMesh. The single number I need is that budget:

   8 near creatures at full mesh   1,792 tris    ~5% M10K
  16                               2,720         ~7%
  32                               4,928        ~12%
  64                               7,936        ~19%

Note: a giant near the camera is unaffected by LOD and needs 25,704 references on its own. Reference capacity must be sized for it regardless.

D3. MEASURE.HISTOGRAM — four laws, or leave it refused

Its contract is a reasoned refusal and I agree with it: "a small block built on four invented laws is worse than no block, because the inventions become ratified by being built." To revive it you would have to rule on:

  1. The error metric — what number goes in a bucket? Projected screen error, per-fragment residual, or per-meshlet deviation? No spec names one, no Q format is declared, and no block currently produces one.
  2. The bucket boundaries — how many, linear or logarithmic, over what range?
  3. The cutoff rule — "a cutoff bucket is selected" is the entire current specification. Selected how, against what budget, with what hysteresis?
  4. A Version-2 input to MEASURE.GOVERNOR, which was just built to Version 1.

My recommendation: leave it refused. It is the only block in the tree whose absence is itself documented reasoning.

D4. What does 276,480 actually count?

Source: reports/PER_PIXEL_BUDGET.md. Decides whether two per-pixel blocks need replicating at all — i.e. whether real work is being sized against a number that means something different from what it is used for.

D5. Three-sample material recipes — confirm the TMU is sized for it

MATERIAL_ARCHITECTURE.md ratifies bounded recipes of up to three samples and names a sacrifice_terrain_3sample profile at 1,094,600 samples a frame. The rearchitecture brief sizes the TMU against 541,640 — which is the one-sample workload. At 3-sample terrain only 238,733 samples remain inside the 20% reserve, and that must cover creatures, objects, cache misses, queue bubbles and integration cost.

Confirm: is three-sample terrain a shipping target, or a capability held in reserve behind the degradation ladder?

E. The 8 km terrain world — separate document, in progress

reports/Missingterrain is the brief and it is excellent. Its own summary: "The terrain engine's organs exist. The circulatory system that feeds them an island does not."

I have started building that layer — the deterministic 1,024-slot residency directory with page generations, dirty tracking and stale-handle protection is written and lint-clean. A full architecture document for the remaining world layer (island directory, prefetch, dirty writeback, the 256-patch composed-height cache, and the command path into the terrain pipeline) is being produced separately, and its open questions will follow in a second brief.

E1. The ten terrain world-layer questions

Architecture document: reports/TERRAIN_WORLD_LAYER_ARCHITECTURE.md. Chain: WORLD.DIRECTORY (HPS) → TERRAIN.RESIDENCY (built) → TERRAIN.PAGELOADER → TERRAIN.WRITEBACK → TERRAIN.COMPCACHE → TERRAIN.SEQ → the existing PATCH/LOD/TESS/NORMALS/PROJECT chain.

One capacity finding, because it constrains several answers: the 256-patch composed height+velocity cache is 256 × 2,178 B × 2 = 8.92 Mbit = 161% of every M10K on the device. It cannot be on-chip. The design stores it in SDRAM with a small on-chip patch front (~16 M10K) feeding TESS's registered port.

  1. Residency key law — world-absolute patch coordinates at one canonical pitch, or (island_id, ix, iz)? The block I already built tags only (px, py) — there is no island_id, so this one is an interface change if it goes the other way.
  2. Guard map — what base and size does each terrain region get in memory_rules §5?
  3. Memory client — a new zhao_client_e id (SUNDER proposes client 6, TERRAIN_BUILD) or reuse ENGINE0? Guaranteed round-robin or best-effort?
  4. Writeback vs canonical mirror — does the FPGA write layers B/D back, or is SW.CPUCOLL's canonical mirror the reload source? And must layer F (no CPU mirror) be written back?
  5. Command ABI — one DrawProcedural-lineage record per visible patch, or a new set-of-patches command?
  6. Composed-cache overflow — past 256 patches, what degrades and in what deterministic order?
  7. Prefetch policy — what look-ahead ring, and what per-frame page budget? (provisional: 32 pages ≈ 41 MB/s)
  8. Mip derivation side — HPS into the staged page, or the FPGA loader at load time?
  9. Visible-set collision law — is thrash merely counted, or must software guarantee it never submits a colliding pair? (the direct map has a 2,048 m period)
  10. Level teardown — is a full reset the ratified unload mechanism, or does the directory need an invalidate port?

Summary of what I need

  1. Three particle/2D/compositor behaviour answers (section B) — unblocks nine blocks at once.
  2. wmin, wmax, scale (D1) — unblocks the renderer's last piece.
  3. The kMesh budget: 8, 16, 32 or 64 (D2) — unblocks the binner arena, which is now affordable.
  4. Is three-sample terrain a shipping target? (D5).
  5. What 276,480 counts (D4).
  6. The ten terrain world-layer answers (E1) — unblocks the 8 km world, the biggest outstanding thing in the console.
  7. Optional: revive MEASURE.HISTOGRAM (D3) — recommend not.

Six answers and one number would make every remaining block in this console buildable. Nothing left is blocked on effort, and nothing is waiting on hardware to arrive.
